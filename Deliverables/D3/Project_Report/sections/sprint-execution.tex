\section{Sprint execution}

The team used Jira sprints to group a portion of the project work into
time-bounded iterations. The four sprints represented in Jira are
\emph{Deliverable 0}, \emph{Sprint 3 (D2)}, \emph{Sprint 3 (D2) 2} and
\emph{Sprint 3 (D3)}. Their recorded start and planned end dates, issue
counts and completion-by-close counts are summarised in
Table~\ref{tab:jira-sprints}. Done by close means that an issue's Jira
resolution date is on or before the sprint's close date; it is not simply the
issue's current status. For the active sprint, the generated table reports
work completed so far.

\paragraph{Deliverable 0.}
The recorded goal was to prepare the one-page project proposal. It covered
the project description and user roles, the core transactional workflow,
the shared resource and concurrency challenge, the intended use of AI agents
and their tools, the proposed technology stack, initial risks and technical
uncertainties, and the initial Jira/GitHub development strategy.

\paragraph{Sprint 3 (D2).}
The recorded goal is \emph{Web app scaffolded and sign-in/registration working
end to end}. The sprint closed on 4 October, four days before its planned end,
with all of its issues done. The table gives the issue and completion counts
from Jira; it is not a complete measure of all D2 work, since some work was
not assigned to a sprint.

\paragraph{Sprint 3 (D2) 2.}
This sprint started as soon as the first D2 sprint closed, and it has no
written goal in its Jira goal field, so its scope is best understood from the
issues assigned to it. It contains further D2 implementation and refinement
work. It stayed open until 28 October, 18 days past its planned end. Assigned
issues and their recorded resolution dates provide the basis for the
completion figures. This second sprint record is retained separately, as it
appears in Jira.

\paragraph{Sprint 3 (D3).}
The recorded goal is \emph{Deliverable 3}. Its issues cover the integration,
adversarial, performance, system, failure and security testing, the defect
history, the deployment, the timeline, the traceability matrix, and the final
dashboard and report. The table gives its figures at the Jira snapshot of
\JiraSnapshotDate{}; while a sprint is open, they show progress rather than a
final sprint-close result.

\paragraph{Planning and review limitations.}
Sprint planning was not applied consistently across the entire project.
Some issues were left without a sprint assignment, so work performed outside
the four recorded sprints is not reflected in the sprint table. Story-point
estimates were also incomplete: \JiraWithEstimate{} of \JiraIssues{} issues
have one, so the team cannot use the Jira records to make a reliable
effort-based velocity comparison. Issue counts are not proxies for equal
effort.

Every issue in the three closed sprints was resolved by the sprint's close, so
the records show no work carried forward. That does not mean the work finished
on plan: Deliverable 0 closed on 1 October against a planned 29 August, and
Sprint 3 (D2) 2 closed on 28 October against a planned 10 October.

The Jira sprint records do not provide a consistent written record of sprint
reviews or retrospectives for every iteration. Accordingly, this section
does not claim that a review or retrospective took place where there is no
recorded evidence. The table and descriptions are limited to sprint metadata
and issue history available in Jira.
