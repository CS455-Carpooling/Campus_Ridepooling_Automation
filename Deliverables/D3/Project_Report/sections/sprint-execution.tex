\section{Sprint execution}

The team used Jira sprints to group a portion of the project work into
time-bounded iterations. The four sprints represented in Jira are
\emph{Deliverable 0}, \emph{Sprint 3 (D2)}, \emph{Sprint 3 (D2) 2} and
\emph{Sprint 3 (D3)}. Their recorded start and planned end dates, issue
counts and completion-by-close counts are summarised in
Table\ref{tab:jira-sprints}. Done by close means that an issue's Jira
resolution date is on or before the sprint's close date; it is not simply the
issue's current status. For the active sprint, the generated table reports

\paragraph{Deliverable 0.}
The recorded goal was to prepare the one-page project proposal. It covered
the project description and user roles, the core transactional workflow,
the shared resource and concurrency challenge, the intended use of AI agents
and their tools, the proposed technology stack, initial risks and technical
uncertainties, and the initial Jira/GitHub development strategy.

\paragraph{Sprint 3 (D2).}
This sprint was associated with Deliverable 2 implementation work. The Jira
sprint record does not contain a written sprint goal, so its scope is best
understood from the issues assigned to it and the work recorded against
those issues. The table gives the issue and completion counts from Jira; it
is not a complete measure of all D2 work, since some work was not assigned
to a sprint.

\paragraph{Sprint 3 (D2) 2.}
The second D2 sprint record also has no written goal in its Jira goal field.
It contains further D2 implementation and refinement work. Assigned issues
and their recorded resolution dates provide the basis for the completion
figures. This second sprint record is retained separately, as it appears
in Jira.

\paragraph{Sprint 3 (D3).}
The recorded goal is \emph{Deliverable 3}. Its issues cover final deliverable
work, including the report and supporting project evidence. At the time
represented by the Jira data, this sprint was active; its completion figure
is therefore a progress figure rather than a final sprint-close result.

\paragraph{Planning and review limitations.}
Sprint planning was not applied consistently across the entire project.
Some issues were left without a sprint assignment, so work performed outside
the four recorded sprints is not reflected in the sprint table. Story-point
estimates were also incomplete: only some issues had estimates, and the team
cannot use the Jira records to make a reliable effort-based velocity
comparison. Issue counts are not proxies for equal effort.

The Jira sprint records do not provide a consistent written record of sprint
reviews or retrospectives for every iteration. Accordingly, this section
does not claim that a review or retrospective took place where there is no
recorded evidence. The table and descriptions are limited to sprint metadata
and issue history available in Jira.
